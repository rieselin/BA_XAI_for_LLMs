This thesis grew out of two converging experiences. The first was my project thesis from the
previous semester, \textit{Increasing Trust in AI Systems}, which examined Explainable AI
methods in the context of image recognition. Working on that project sparked a broader
question that the image domain alone could not answer: how much should people actually trust
the outputs of Large Language Models, and what can be done to help them find out? The
second experience was more personal. Conversations with people in my immediate environment
who do not have a technical background made it increasingly clear that the gap between how
confidently LLMs present their outputs and how reliably those outputs can be trusted is not
well understood by most users. These discussions convinced me that the question of trust
calibration in LLMs was not only academically interesting but practically urgent.

The path from that motivation to a finished study was not without obstacles. The survey
platform UniPark experienced outages during the data collection period, creating uncertainty
at a critical stage of the project, and getting Chain-of-Thought extraction to work reliably
within the constraints of a locally hosted open-source model required considerably more
iteration than anticipated. Both challenges ultimately led to more robust solutions than the
ones originally planned.
